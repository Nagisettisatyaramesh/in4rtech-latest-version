## Services

**Q: What is included in your Enterprise Architecture service?**

## Challenges we solve

**Q: What problems do you help organisations solve?**
A: Common challenges include legacy technology estates, disconnected systems, fragmented data, manual and repetitive processes, unclear AI opportunities, technology decisions made without a long-term architecture, applications that are hard to modernise, and the lack of a practical roadmap.

**Q: Why do AI pilots fail to scale?**
A: A pilot proves a model can work, but scaling depends on data access and quality, integration into real processes, clear ownership and governance. These are architecture questions. Treating AI as part of the wider technology landscape turns a successful experiment into something the business can depend on. Read more on our Insights page.

## Approach

**Q: What is your approach? / How does an engagement work?**
A: Most engagements follow six stages, in full or in part: Discover (understand goals and the current landscape), Assess (analyse architecture, applications, data and processes), Architect (define the target state and a sequenced roadmap), Enable (prioritise where AI and automation add value), Transform (support implementation and adoption) and Evolve (review and refine as things change). Every engagement is shaped around where the organisation is starting from.

**Q: What does an Enterprise Architecture review deliver?**
A: A typical six-week review runs: Week 1 Discover, Weeks 2–3 Assess, Week 4 Target state, Week 5 Roadmap, Week 6 Playback. You end with a clear current-state view, a target architecture and guiding principles, rationalisation options, a sequenced roadmap including where AI and automation fit, and a short list of decisions for leadership. Every plan is tailored to the organisation.

**Q: Should we automate our processes as they are?**
A: Usually not straight away. Automating a broken process makes it fail faster. We recommend simplifying the process, fixing integration gaps and deciding where human judgement matters first, then automating the repetitive, rules-based work that remains.
